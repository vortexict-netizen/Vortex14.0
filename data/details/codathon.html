Codathon 14.0

A marine-inspired coding adventure testing creativity, logic, and problem-solving skills


Back to Events

Prize Pool & Info

  • Prize Pool: ₹25,000
  • Offline rounds will be held at ICT Mumbai campus. Dates TBA. Exact details provided post-registration.

Format & Guidelines

Event Overview: Codathon 14.0 brings a fresh, marine-inspired adventure designed to test creativity, logic, and problem-solving skills through light-hearted challenges. Instead of high-pressure competition, participants navigate interesting logical obstacles and surface with clever solutions.

Philosophy: This event addresses a growing concern: many rely on LLMs to write code, which degenerates problem-solving skills and logical thinking. Programming has always been about integrating science, engineering, coding, logic, and willingness to struggle through ideas. We want to create a space where participants can genuinely test their abilities.

Participation: Individual event only. Participants may bring their own laptops (optional). Lab computers might not have tools for every language.

Important: Using AI tools such as ChatGPT is strictly prohibited and leads to immediate disqualification. False identity leads to immediate disqualification.

Rules & Rounds

Round 1: Fundamentals

Duration: 1 to 1.5 hours

Question Structure:

  • ~7 simple logical MCQs, carrying 1 mark each
  • ~6 questions assessing basic programming skills, carrying 3 marks each

Expected Skills:

  • Debugging Skills: Basic programming error (wrt datatypes, input variables and arrays), and basic logical errors while formulating algorithms.
  • Improving/Refining Code: Reducing time, reducing the amount of unnecessary computations, and optimizing algorithms (reducing redundant steps).
  • Application: Translating basic engineering ideas into code/algorithm, and the ability to fit or construct a tiny model.

Round 2: Deep Problem Solving

Duration: ~4 hours total (3-4 questions, ~1 hour each)

Marks Distribution per Question:

  • 1 mark for final answer
  • 2 marks for code presentation and readability
  • ~5 marks for logic, reasoning, and engineering thought process
  • ~2 marks for programming/implementation prowess

Resources Available: You'll get all theory you need, plus internet access: books, blogs, lecture notes, Stack Overflow, and forums.

Expected Skills:

  • How you formulate a plan of attack/approach for the problem, breaking it down into manageable pieces.
  • Ability to pick the right tasks, simplify or postpone others, and make clear decisions that maximize outcome in limited time.
  • Make reasoning explicit in the source: comment on each major step with a short justification explaining why you chose it.
  • Effectively turn conceptual understanding and constraints into structured algorithms and computational models.
  • How you frame a problem often determines how well you solve it.

Language Choice: Use whichever programming language you want. Assembly is preferred the most. Of course, if we observe you writing a solution directly as machine code (and it's correct) - you get a chocolate!

Code Quality and Evaluation

Evaluation Criteria:

  • Judges' decisions are final (unless they change their mind - rare but theoretically possible).
  • Evaluation goes beyond output – we'll run your code and review your source file to assess approach and efficiency.

Code Quality Requirements:

  • Add comments to clarify your logic; all steps in approach and algorithm require proper and sufficient justification.
  • Comment in a systematic and brief way to maximize understanding about your approach.
  • Use clear, concise names and keep code neat and readable.
  • Clean formatting, organized blocks, and sensible flow make approach easier to understand.
  • A good solution is both correct and understandable.

Submission:

  • Attach a PDF/Word file of your code in case source file is corrupted.

Event Instructions

Before the Event:

  • Arrive 20-30 minutes early to settle in and ensure your editor, compiler, and runtime are all set.
  • Participants may bring their own laptops (optional). Lab computers might not have tools for every language.

During the Event:

  • Limited Python-only troubleshooting support available, depending on manpower. This is for technical issues, not problem-solving help.
  • Stay true to the spirit of the event: solve honestly, think for yourself, and keep things fair.

General Guidelines:

  • Treat volunteers and judges with respect. They're here to help, not to argue.
  • Think calmly, breathe, and let your creativity shine - don't be a bot.
  • False identity will lead to immediate disqualification.
  • Using AI tools such as ChatGPT is strictly prohibited and leads to immediate disqualification.
