Moot Court

Debate on pharmacy laws, ethics, and regulations in a courtroom setting

Format & Guidelines

Event Overview: Do you enjoy diving into legal and pharma discussions? Moot Court is designed to spread awareness about patent laws, ethics, regulatory policies, pharmacy legislations, and the working of the FDA.

What is a Moot Court? A simulated courtroom setting where participants take on the role of advocates and present legal arguments before a panel of judges. Competitors will receive a detailed moot problem, analyse, research, and prepare arguments for both prosecution and defence.

Team Format: Each team will consist of two members.

Key Skills Evaluated: Public speaking, research methodology, structured presentation, critical thinking, and communication strength.

Rules & Rounds

Round 1: Online Quiz

Round 1 will be conducted in the form of an online quiz designed to test both accuracy and conceptual clarity. It includes 30 MCQs, each carrying +4 marks for a correct answer, –1 for an incorrect one, and 0 for unattempted questions. The total marks for this round are 120.

Tie-breaking Criteria:

  • If two teams score the same marks, the team with fewer incorrect answers will be preferred. If still unresolved, the team that finishes the quiz earlier will be selected.
  • In Round 1, only one email ID from each team will be registered for the quiz. Although both participants are allowed to discuss answers together, the quiz must be attempted from a single device using the registered email ID.
  • This round is designed to test not only your knowledge of pharma laws, ethics, regulations, and legal fundamentals, but also your team coordination and communication.
  • As part of the verification, participants will also be required to enter their partner's full name during the quiz. Any mismatch or incorrect information may lead to disqualification.

Round 2: Debate Round

Round 2 brings participants into a moot court-style debate arena. Approximately 12 teams will qualify for this stage.

Structure: This will be a panel debate where multiple teams argue from different assigned perspectives. The problem statement will be released only after the round begins, and teams will be randomly assigned either 'for' or 'against' the motion.

Debate Format:

  • Opening Statement: Each team presents their position clearly and concisely.
  • Main Arguments: Teams expand on their stance with well-researched points supported by legal principles, pharmaceutical knowledge, evidence, and case studies.
  • Rebuttal: Teams critically address and dismantle opposing arguments using logic and factual support.
  • Closing Statement: Brief and impactful summary of the team's strongest points.

Judging Criteria:

  • Content Knowledge: Depth, accuracy, and relevance of research.
  • Argumentation: Strength, clarity, organization, and logical flow.
  • Rebuttal Quality: Effectiveness in countering opposing points.
  • Presentation Skills: Confidence, articulation, clarity, poise, and teamwork.
  • Legal & Pharma Knowledge: Understanding of relevant laws, regulations, and ethical dimensions.

Round 3: Clash of Convictions

The final round brings the top 6 teams into an intense, courtroom-inspired debate enriched with multimedia evidence.

Format: Participants will argue on a pre-released problem statement, but must prepare for both sides of the motion, as their stance (For/Against) will be assigned at the beginning of the round.

Multimedia Use: Teams may use slides, videos, images, data, and other visual aids to reinforce their arguments.

Debate Structure:

  • Opening Arguments: Clear introduction of team's assigned stance.
  • Evidence Presentation: Use of credible and relevant multimedia evidence to support claims.
  • Rebuttals: Logical counterarguments addressing opposing points.
  • Closing Statements: Concise summarization of strongest arguments.

Dress Code: All participants must adhere to formal attire, maintaining seriousness and authenticity of courtroom setting.

Evaluation Criteria:

  • Understanding of Legal Principles: Grasp of regulations, case facts, and both sides of issue.
  • Presentation Skills: Confidence, clarity, coherence, and structured delivery.
  • Strength of Evidence: Relevance, credibility, accuracy, and effective integration of multimedia.
  • Rebuttal Quality: Ability to counter opposing arguments with logic and precision.
  • Professionalism: Body language, time management, and overall conduct.

Rules for Round 3:

  • Plagiarism of evidence strictly prohibited and will lead to immediate disqualification.
  • Disrespectful behaviour, offensive language, or any form of disruption will result in penalties.
  • Judges' decision is final and binding.
  • Audience can participate, extra points awarded for meaningful audience engagement.
  • A maximum of 6 teams compete in Round 3, with 3 moot courts conducted on 3 different cases. Consequently, there will be 3 winning teams (subject to change based on eliminations).

General Rules

  • During all three rounds, participants not allowed to access external information sources (internet, library, notes, etc.). Use of multimedia allowed only to gather information for presentation purpose in Round 3.
  • All decisions taken by authorities, judges, or event heads are final and binding.
  • Speaking order of mooters will be predefined.
  • Each team can have maximum of two mooters, with each mooter allotted 5 minutes to speak.
  • Judges may pause timer at any moment to ask questions or seek clarifications.
  • Opposing team cannot respond or question during presenting team's 5-minute window.
  • After both teams complete their proposals, a 10-minute debate segment will follow for interactive discussion.
  • In case of any disputes, decision of event heads and overall event head shall be final and binding.
